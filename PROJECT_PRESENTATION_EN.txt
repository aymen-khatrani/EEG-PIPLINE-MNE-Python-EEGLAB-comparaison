================================================================================
1. ONE-SENTENCE SUMMARY
================================================================================

The project reproduces in Python (MNE library) a preprocessing pipeline for EEG
signals recorded simultaneously with functional MRI (EEG-fMRI), a pipeline
originally built in EEGLAB (MATLAB); it adds a machine-learning component to
classify the ICA components, a set of machine-learning-inspired alternatives to
the classical artifact-correction steps, and a quantitative comparison between the
two implementations.


================================================================================
2. CONTEXT AND PROBLEM STATEMENT
================================================================================

Simultaneous EEG-fMRI combines two complementary modalities:
  - EEG (electroencephalography), very precise in TIME (milliseconds);
  - functional MRI (fMRI), very precise in SPACE (millimeters).

Recording EEG inside the MRI scanner raises a major problem: the EEG signal is
massively contaminated by two artifacts:
  - the gradient artifact, caused by the fast switching of the MRI gradients; its
    amplitude is on the order of a millivolt, i.e. about 1000 times the useful EEG,
    which is on the order of a microvolt;
  - the ballistocardiogram (BCG) or pulse artifact, caused by the movement of the
    electrodes induced by the heartbeat inside the magnetic field.

Historically, cleaning these artifacts is done in EEGLAB (MATLAB) with the FMRIB
plugin (FASTR algorithm for the gradient and OBS for the BCG).

Limitations of this approach: MATLAB is proprietary and paid software, and the
manual pipeline is poorly reproducible and hard to automate.

Hence the problem statement: can we faithfully reproduce this pipeline in Python
(free, open, reproducible), validate it quantitatively against EEGLAB, and add a
modern machine-learning component?


================================================================================
3. GOALS AND OBJECTIVES
================================================================================

Scientific and technical objectives:
  O1. Reproduce from scratch, in Python/MNE, each step of the EEGLAB pipeline,
      including the FASTR (gradient) and OBS (BCG) algorithms which do not exist
      natively in MNE.
  O2. Build a machine-learning dataset describing the ICA components (feature
      extraction) and label it.
  O3. Select and optimize a machine-learning model to classify the components
      (artifact vs neural), and compare it to ICLabel, the domain reference tool.
  O4. Quantitatively compare the EEGLAB and Python outputs (spectrum, wavelets, ICA
      topographies, statistics) to prove the equivalence of the pipelines.

Bonus objective: test whether machine-learning-inspired alternatives (PCA/Ridge
regression, non-linear MLP autoencoder) improve the classical artifact-correction
steps (AAS for the gradient, OBS for the BCG).

ISIA competency objectives validated by the project:
  - Objective 1: mobilize advanced scientific and technical knowledge (signal
    processing, multivariate statistics, non-parametric tests).
  - Objective 2: define and build machine-learning datasets.
  - Objective 3: select a model and optimize its parameters.


================================================================================
4. DATA USED
================================================================================

  - Subject: sub-38 (real laboratory data).
  - EEG: 32 channels, including 31 EEG electrodes in the 10-20 system and 1 ECG
    channel.
  - Sampling rate: 5000 Hz.
  - Recording: inside the MRI scanner, resting-state task (rest).
  - Duration: about 392 seconds per recording.
  - Number of recordings: 2 (run-01 and run-02).
  - The notebook also runs on a simulated dataset, which makes it executable without

================================================================================
5. THE PARTS OF THE PROJECT
================================================================================

The project is organized into five phases.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 0 - Configuration and loading
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Centralization of all parameters in a single configuration cell (including the
parameters of the machine-learning comparison methods), and loading of the
BrainVision data (or generation of a simulated dataset). The ECG channel is marked
as such so as not to pollute the EEG processing.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 1 - Preprocessing (the core of the project)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Faithful chaining of the EEGLAB steps:
  1.2  Automatic detection of the fMRI volume trigger from the signal alone
       (data-driven approach based on the periodicity of the markers).
  1.3  Gradient-artifact correction by AAS (Average Artifact Subtraction), the
       in-house equivalent of FASTR.
  1.3bis  BONUS - method comparison for the gradient (see section 6).
  1.4  50 Hz notch filter (mains power).
  1.5  QRS complex detection on the ECG (heartbeat landmarks).
  1.6  BCG correction by OBS (Optimal Basis Set), the in-house equivalent of
       FASTR/OBS.
  1.6bis  BONUS - method comparison for the BCG (see section 6).
  1.7  Resampling to 250 Hz.
  1.8  Common average reference (ECG excluded).
  1.9  Band-pass filter 1-40 Hz.
  1.10 ICA by extended Infomax (equivalent of runica in EEGLAB).
  1.11 Automatic component classification by ICLabel.
  1.12 Removal of artifact components and reconstruction of the clean signal.
  1.13 Batch processing of the other recordings with exactly the same steps.

Concrete results obtained on sub-38:
  - Volume trigger detected: R128, TR of about 3 seconds, 120 volumes.
  - 767 heartbeats detected.
  - 30 ICA components (one fewer than 31 channels, because of the average reference).
  - 5 of the 30 components identified as artifacts (blinks, muscle, etc.).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 2 - EEGLAB vs Python comparison
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
We do not assume the equivalence of the two pipelines, we measure it. The notebook
provides two levels:
  - Per-axis checks (2.1 to 2.4): power spectrum (Welch), time-frequency (Morlet
    wavelets), structural ICA-topography matching (by spatial correlation, since
    ICA is not deterministic), and inferential statistics (Wilcoxon, permutation,
    FDR correction).
  - A full quantitative comparison (2.5) driven by an EEGLAB export, organized in
    four sub-sections:
      2.5.0  load the EEGLAB export (.set + .mat) and shared helpers;
      2.5.1  stage-by-stage PSD (import, gradient, notch, BCG, filter, clean) -
             a per-stage correlation above 0.8 shows the two implementations act
             the same way at each step;
      2.5.2  final-signal equivalence (per-channel PSD correlation, per-band
             differences, spatial topography, occipital alpha peak);
      2.5.3  ICA + ICLabel agreement (optimal Hungarian matching of the two
             component sets, Cohen kappa on the labels);
      2.5.4  an automatic PASS/CHECK verdict.
  This section runs only if the EEGLAB export is present; otherwise it prints a
  hint and is skipped. The per-axis cells (2.1/2.3) are placeholders ready to
  receive the same export.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 3 - Machine learning: ICA-component classification
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  - Extraction of 12 features per component (spectral, temporal, spatial).
  - Labeling from ICLabel.
  - Generation of synthetic components to augment the dataset.
  - Training and comparison of 4 model families (logistic regression, random forest,
    SVM, gradient boosting) with cross-validation and GridSearchCV.
  - Comparison to the reference model ICLabel via Cohen's kappa.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PHASE 4 - Downstream analysis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Computation of the power per frequency band over time (band power) on the cleaned
signal. This proves that the pipeline output is directly usable scientifically, and
serves as a final quality check.


================================================================================
6. BONUS - MACHINE-LEARNING METHOD COMPARISON (Phase 1bis)
================================================================================

Beyond reproducing AAS and OBS, the notebook asks a research question: would a model
richer than the classical template help? For both the gradient and the BCG, the
current method is compared to three alternatives in the same "template regression"
family but with an increasingly expressive basis, using two metrics: template-
residual reduction (higher is better) and alpha-power preservation (close to 1 means
the neural signal is kept, not destroyed).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1  Gradient (section 1.3bis): AAS vs PCA / Ridge / MLP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Methods compared:
  - AAS (current): single sliding-average template.
  - PCA regression: optimal basis of principal components (same idea as OBS).
  - Ridge regression: enlarged basis + regularized regression.
  - MLP autoencoder: non-linear bottleneck (neural network) instead of a linear
    projection.

Result: the four methods are practically identical (RMS reduction 95.8-96.5 %,
template-residual reduction 99.9-100 %). This is an informative negative result: the
gradient artifact is about 1000 times larger than the EEG, so even the simplest
template already saturates the achievable performance, leaving no room for a richer
model to help.
Note: the alpha-preservation metric is not interpretable for the gradient, because
the "before" reference is contaminated by spectral leakage of the huge artifact.
Conclusion: keep the simple AAS - as good as the costlier alternatives, and the
fastest.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.2  BCG (section 1.6bis): OBS vs OBS-auto / Ridge-on-ECG / MLP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Methods compared:
  - OBS (current): 4 fixed principal components per channel.
  - OBS-auto: number of components chosen automatically per channel by explained
    variance (data-driven rank).
  - Ridge on ECG reference: regression of each EEG channel on the ECG and its
    derivative (external, causal reference; close to adaptive noise cancellation).
  - MLP autoencoder: non-linear bottleneck on the same internal basis as the OBS.

Result (this is the key bonus finding):
  - OBS-auto (mean 8 components vs 4) barely gains in RMS but does worse on the two
    metrics that matter (template reduction 42.3 % vs 44.3 %, alpha preservation
    0.845 vs 0.926): classic overfitting.
  - Ridge on ECG reference removes almost nothing (RMS reduction 0.118 %): a linear
    combination of the ECG is too poor a template.
  - MLP autoencoder nearly DOUBLES the residual removal (RMS reduction 50.1 % vs
    21.2 %; template reduction 78.7 % vs 44.3 %) while preserving alpha almost as
    well (0.907 vs 0.926). This reveals a real non-linear structure in the beat-to-
    beat variability of the BCG that the linear OBS misses.

Conclusion: unlike the gradient, non-linearity makes a measurable, coherent
difference for the BCG - the MLP autoencoder clearly outperforms the current OBS.
Caveat: this is a single-subject result; it must be confirmed on sub-37/sub-41
before replacing the OBS in the main chain.


================================================================================
7. KEY RESULT OF PHASE 3 - AN OVERFITTING UNMASKED
================================================================================

A first version mixed real and synthetic components during training then re-evaluated
on the real components. It gave very high scores (AUC close to 0.999, kappa of 0.71).
These scores were MISLEADING for two reasons:
  - data leakage: the real test components had been seen during training;
  - trivial test set: dominated by very easily separable synthetic components.

The protocol was corrected: training on synthetic data only, testing on the 30 real
components never seen (out-of-sample). Honest results:
  - training AUC, cross-validation AUC and synthetic test AUC = 1.00;
  - but real out-of-sample AUC = only 0.50 to 0.67 (close to chance);
  - agreement with ICLabel = accuracy 0.53, kappa 0.067 (negligible).

Interpretation: the idealized synthetic components do not capture the real
variability. The model had overfit the artificial structure of the synthetic data;
it does not transfer to the real data. This is a NEGATIVE but INFORMATIVE result: a
credible classifier requires real annotated components, in sufficient number and
across several subjects.


================================================================================
8. ASSUMED LIMITATIONS
================================================================================

  - Gradient: simplified in-house AAS (not the full FASTR regularization).
  - BCG: simplified in-house OBS (no baseline-drift correction).
  - Non-deterministic ICA: structural comparison, not point by point.
  - Small N: a single subject; machine learning and the bonus method comparison are
    not robust at this scale (results to confirm multi-subject).
  - Synthetic-to-real transfer does not work for the ICA classifier; real
    multi-subject annotations are needed.
  - Spectral comparisons: align windowing and units before any conclusion.


================================================================================
9. CONCLUSION AND PERSPECTIVES
================================================================================

What has been achieved:
  - A complete, reproducible and open EEG-fMRI pipeline, faithfully reproduced in
    Python, with the FASTR and OBS algorithms coded from scratch.
  - A QUANTIFIED EEGLAB vs Python equivalence (2.5), run on a real EEGLAB export:
    per-stage PSD correlation up to 0.96, alpha peak match (7.7 vs 7.1 Hz, PASS),
    ICA topography median |r| = 0.61, ICLabel agreement kappa = 0.58 (PASS).
  - A rigorous machine-learning component, with a sound evaluation protocol that
    unmasks over-optimism.
  - A bonus method comparison showing where machine learning helps (BCG) and where it
    does not (gradient).

Perspectives (genuinely open items):
  - Manually annotate the real components (a true expert ground truth, more credible
    than the ICLabel silver standard). This is NOT done yet.
  - Extend the quantified EEGLAB comparison (2.5) to run-02 and to more subjects.
  - Aggregate several subjects for robust machine learning, valid statistical tests,
    and to confirm the MLP-vs-OBS bonus result.
  - Make the synthetic-data generator more realistic.
